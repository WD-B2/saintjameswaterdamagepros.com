What local conditions affect a job

The Suffolk County Hazard Mitigation Plan describes groundwater flooding as a concern across the Town of Smithtown, and FEMA flood maps do not capture it. That matters for basements and crawl spaces, where water can rise without a river or storm surge nearby. Our basement page covers how crews deal with it.

The National Weather Service flash flood emergency of August 2024 named Smithtown and Stony Brook after 6 to 10 inches of rain overnight. Storm response is covered on the storm water damage page.

Permit and building offices by town

Repair permits go to different offices depending on where the house stands. Saint James, Nesconset and Smithtown fall under the Town of Smithtown Building Department. Lake Grove and Islandia are incorporated villages with their own building departments.

Ask the right office whether your repair needs a permit before work begins. The town pages linked above name the correct office for each place.

Beyond the four towns

Nearby places such as Stony Brook and Port Jefferson Station are also within reach of the crews we dispatch. If you live elsewhere in northern Suffolk County, call the dispatch line and give your address.

A commercial building in the area can be covered too. See commercial water damage restoration for how shops and offices are handled.

Picking the right service

Whatever your town, start with the cause. Water from a pipe, an appliance, the weather or a sewer line each calls for different steps. The services index groups every page by cause, so you can find yours in a few seconds.

What to have ready when you call

Give the dispatcher your street address and the nearest cross street. Mention whether the property is a house, an apartment or a commercial space. If you know the source of the water, say so. If you do not, describe where it first appeared.

That information lets the crew bring the right equipment on the first trip, which saves time when water is still spreading.

Why distance does not change the plan

Distances on this page are straight-line estimates and are not a promise of arrival time. Whether you are in Saint James or Islandia, the sequence is the same: stop the source, extract water, measure moisture and dry the structure. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

Water districts and utilities nearby

Water service in the area comes from local water districts, and which one supplies your home depends on your street. Check a recent bill if you are unsure. If a main line or meter is leaking, the district is the one to shut it off, and our slab leak page explains how to check your meter.

For electric safety after flooding, PSEG Long Island advises against entering a flooded room where water has reached outlets, appliances or cords.
