Why the first day matters

Water travels. A supply line leak in a kitchen wall can move into the subfloor, the ceiling below and the baseboards around the next room. Wet drywall and carpet pad start to hold moisture that is slow to leave, which is why drying starts as soon as standing water is gone.

Mold can begin to grow on damp organic materials within a day or two, so every hour of delay widens the area that needs to be opened up. That is the practical reason to call before you start mopping.

  • Stop the source if you can do so safely, such as the main shutoff valve.
  • Keep out of any room where water has reached outlets, appliances or cords.
  • Move valuables and furniture legs off wet flooring.
  • Photograph the damage before you clean anything up.

What is water mitigation and when is it used

Water mitigation is the emergency phase of a job. It covers stopping further loss, extracting water, setting drying equipment and protecting contents. Restoration follows, and that means rebuilding drywall, flooring and finishes once the structure is dry.

Many homeowners hear both terms from an insurer. Mitigation is usually the first step because it limits how much has to be rebuilt later. Ask for the two phases to be described separately in the scope you receive.

Water categories and what they mean

Industry guidance such as ANSI/IICRC S500 groups water by contamination level. The category changes what can be dried in place and what has to be discarded.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it usually means
Category 1Supply line, tank overflow, rainwater from a clean sourceOften dried in place if addressed quickly
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, aquarium overflowCarpet pad and porous items are often removed
Category 3Sewage, river or stormwater flooding, long-standing waterPorous materials removed, areas disinfected, protective gear required

Drying a house the right way

A fan in the window is not drying. Professional drying pairs air movers with LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers and measures progress daily with moisture meters. Typical drying takes several days depending on materials, but the readings, not the calendar, decide when equipment comes out.

Thermal imaging cameras help locate wet areas behind walls and under floors, so crews open only what is needed. Our structural drying page explains the equipment in more detail.

What affects the cost of water damage restoration

Cost depends on the job, so a real number comes from a scope and not from a web page. These are the factors that move it most.

  • Water category and how long the water sat
  • Square footage and number of rooms affected
  • Materials involved, such as hardwood, carpet, drywall or insulation
  • Days of drying and the amount of equipment needed
  • How much rebuild work follows the drying

Ask for the scope in writing

Scope and price are explained before work starts. Ask what is being removed, what is being dried, and what is left for a separate rebuild.

Insurance claims

Water damage and your insurance claim

Document first, then call your insurer

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your own policy or ask your insurer which applies.

New York DFS advises policyholders to file claims as soon as possible and to document losses before cleaning up. Dispatched crews record moisture readings and photos so you have something concrete to share.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Saint James?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, every day. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and no arrival time is promised until a crew is actually assigned. Shut off the water at the main valve and stay out of wet rooms with live outlets while you wait.

What is the first thing to do when I find water damage?

Stop the source if it is safe, usually at the main shutoff valve. Keep out of rooms where water has reached outlets or cords, move valuables off wet floors and photograph everything before cleaning. Then call (934) 223-1875 so crews can start extraction.

How long does it take to dry a house after water damage?

Drying commonly takes several days, but it depends on the materials and the category of water. Crews check moisture readings daily and remove equipment only when materials reach dry standards.

Will my homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Sudden and accidental losses, such as a burst pipe, are often covered. Gradual leaks, neglect and flooding generally are not under a standard policy. Read your policy or ask your insurer, and document the damage before cleaning.

Can wet drywall and carpet be saved?

Sometimes. Clean water caught quickly can often be dried in place. Contaminated water, or materials that stayed wet for days, are usually removed. Moisture meters and thermal imaging guide the decision.
