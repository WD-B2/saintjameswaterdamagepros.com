What the county plan says about flooding in Islandia

The Suffolk County Hazard Mitigation Plan states that no structures in Islandia lie in the floodplain and that no buildings were flood-damaged in Hurricane Sandy, although trees fell. The village joined the National Flood Insurance Program in October 2009. For you, the likely cause of a wet floor is a roof opened by wind, a plumbing failure or heavy runoff at a doorway.

That is useful when you describe the problem on the phone. Tell us if branches hit the house, if the water is clear or dirty and whether it came from above or below. The answer sets whether crews bring ceiling tools, extractors or containment gear.

Before we arrive in Islandia

Wind, branches and heavy rain tend to hit more than one part of a house at once. Work through these cards in order and stop at any that mean standing in water.

1

Keep clear of the stained ceiling

If branches or rain opened the roof, move furniture and electronics out from under the stain. Do not poke at a bulging ceiling yourself, and leave overhead light fixtures off.

2

Close the main valve if a pipe is involved

A water line will keep running until the main is closed. Turn it clockwise, then run a faucet on the lowest floor to relieve pressure in the lines.

3

Cover the opening only if it is reachable and safe

A tarp or heavy plastic sheeting held down with boards can slow rain. Never climb a wet roof or work near downed lines, and if a tree has pulled wires, stay away and call the utility.

4

Lift valuables and photograph everything

Raise electronics and documents off the floor, then photograph the damage before cleanup. New York's Department of Financial Services advises documenting losses first.

5

Plan for an outage

Drying equipment needs electricity, so tell the dispatcher if your power is off. Text SuffolkAlerts to 67283 to get county emergency alerts, which helps when storms take down your internet.

Permits go through the Village of Islandia Building Department

Even though Islandia lies in the Town of Islip, repair permits go through the village Building Department, which issues permits and inspects work. The village's inspector also serves as its floodplain administrator under the NFIP. Ask the Village of Islandia Building Department if your repair needs a permit before replacing wiring, plumbing or framing.

Keep your moisture readings, invoices and photographs together. An inspector or an insurer may ask for them, and a clean record shortens the back and forth.

Ponds, a river and what to check after heavy rain

Honeysuckle Pond and the Connetquot River sit in Lakeland Park. The ground near ponds and rivers tends to stay wet after heavy rain, so look at your foundation, window wells and yard grading. If water pools against the house, a basement or crawl space may take it in.

For a lower level that has taken water, water extraction is the first step, because dry air cannot do its job over standing water. If it is dirty and you suspect a drain problem, read our note on septic and sewer safety in the FAQs below before touching anything.

If the neighborhood loses power or housing

The county plan names the Islandia Mall parking lot and Johnson Avenue Park at Lakeland Avenue as post-disaster housing sites, and notes that Village Hall lacks permanent backup power. Those are long-term recovery details, not a place to dry your home or shelter overnight.

Will a road closure on the LIE delay my crew?

It can. The county plan records that flash flooding closed the LIE at exit 57 in Islandia in August 2014. We do not promise an arrival time for that reason, and the dispatcher will tell you how to protect the house until the crew gets there.

Is my Islandia home in a flood zone?

The county plan says no structures in the village lie in the floodplain, and the village joined the NFIP in October 2009. That does not stop pipe failures or roof leaks. Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood, so ask your insurer about separate flood coverage, which typically takes 30 days to start.

Does insurance cover a tree that damaged my roof?

Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, and gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy, ask your insurer and file as soon as possible, which is what New York's Department of Financial Services advises. Photograph the damage before cleaning up.

Which office handles permits in Islandia?

The village has its own Building Department that issues permits and inspects work. Ask the Village of Islandia Building Department if your repair needs a permit, rather than calling the Town of Islip first.

What if my drains are slow after a flood?

Avoid using toilets and washing machines, and do not have a septic tank pumped if floodwater covers it, per NYS Department of Health guidance. Wear rubber boots, waterproof gloves and an N95, and never mix bleach with ammonia. Find out if your home is on a sewer or a private system.
